Master's thesis, University of Padua, October 2026

Jet physics of MG3 J163554+3629 and its impact on supermassive black hole evolution in the early universe

Supervisor: Prof. Eugenio Bottacini. Co-supervisor: Dr. Andrea Tramacere.

Blazars point their jets at us, so they are bright enough to see at high redshift and let us study black holes in the first billion years. I modelled the broadband SED of MG3 J163554+3629, one of the most distant blazars detected by Fermi-LAT, adding recent NuSTAR hard X-ray data. I used a one-zone leptonic model in JetSeT over a grid of accretion efficiencies η = 0.057–0.3, which covers black-hole spins from a = 0 to near-maximal.

  • Black-hole mass MBH ≈ 1.8 × 10⁹ M⊙ and Eddington ratio λ ≈ 0.08, for an inner disk radius of 8 RS.
  • The NuSTAR band is a mix of synchrotron self-Compton and external Compton emission. The γ-rays come from Compton scattering of dusty-torus photons.
  • The jet is particle-dominated and carries more power than the disk radiates.
  • Constant growth at the observed rate needs a seed of ≳ 10⁷ M⊙ at z = 30, heavier than any direct-collapse seed. The black hole must have had an earlier phase of faster accretion at low radiative efficiency.
